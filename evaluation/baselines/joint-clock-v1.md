# Exact joint clock reference, v1

This evaluation-only decoder searches the original two frame heads. Its input
contains no truth timestamps, candidate peak list, tempo sections, bar phase or
supplied path. It does not replace the default estimator, run model training,
open the holdout, or establish real-music accuracy.

## Search graph and evidence

Every available run is a finite acyclic semi-Markov graph. A complete bar has
2–7 beats, each independently taking an integer duration of 10–75 frames
(40–300 BPM at 50 Hz). Meter can change between bars and duration can change
at **any beat**, including inside a bar. The first complete bar starts at any
eligible frame; a shared cell boundary is the inferred beat location. Phase is
searched through these boundaries, not supplied as the old phase-4 fixture.

Each complete beat cell and each complete bar cell contributes the frozen
rotation-normalized `[1,2,1]/4` pulse evidence at its first frame. Beat cells
partition their covered beat-head domain; bar cells partition the bar-head
domain. The downbeat head is scored over whole bars, not just rewarded at
selected beat positions. First-frame cyclic smoothing is a modeling assumption,
not access to frames outside a cell. The incremental normalizer is checked
against the previous frozen scorer.

The duration transition weight is `exp(-ln(100) * abs(log2(p/q)))`, with each
outgoing row normalized. The factor is carried from the earlier frozen clock
reference, not tuned against these controls. First duration is uniform over
10–75; every new bar chooses its meter uniformly over 2–7. No fixed section
length or authored tempo is given to this graph.

The initial prefix is uniform over eligible offsets shorter than the maximum
bar length (525 frames). The terminal suffix is also shorter than 525 frames.
Only paths with at least one complete bar are eligible. These bounded edge
rules are an explicit limitation: short input and incomplete edge bars do not
receive complete timing coverage. The entire prefix/suffix is generated by the
same null reference and contributes likelihood ratio one; it is not silently
excluded from a conditional coverage comparison.

For finite graph paths `h`, prior weights `w(h)` and path log evidence `S(h)`:

```
Z0 = sum_h w(h)
Zx = sum_h w(h) * exp(S(h))
clock log ratio to null = log(Zx) - log(Z0)
```

`Z0` conditions the prior on the same finite graph and observed run length.
Searching more hypotheses does not add their maxima as evidence: this is a
prior-weighted **mean** likelihood ratio. A separate max-product pass within
the same dynamic program records a MAP path for diagnosis. Its score is not
the component evidence; its conditional log probability is reported separately.
Uniform flat heads produce zero component evidence regardless of path count.
The null wins ties (numerical tolerance `1e-9`, not a tuned detector threshold).

The sum-product and max-product transitions use two exact L1 sweeps on
log-period coordinates, excluding the duplicate center. Tests compare these
with all-pairs transitions and compare the complete graph's two partitions and
MAP score with independent exhaustive enumeration on a small domain. There
is no beam, data-dependent path pruning or truth-derived restriction.

## Uncertainty and limitations

- Missing frame availability splits the graph into independent runs. No path
  crosses an unavailable interval, and original frame offsets are preserved.
- Flat *available* cells remain in the graph with neutral evidence. Their MAP
  ticks are `prior_only`, not recovered detector observations. The head may
  remain ambiguous even when the component favors some clock over the null.
- All `map_ticks` are diagnostic inferred states, including when the null wins.
  They are not the product's observed beats or calibrated event probabilities.
- `map_complete_bar_span` and `edge_reference_frames` expose MAP edge coverage.
  Other paths in the marginal can cover different edges. An edge is modeled
  under the null, not declared physically silent, and its tempo is not filled in.
- The likelihood-ratio interpretation assumes a common rotation-invariant
  frame reference, sufficient here as an iid product within and across the two
  heads. Actual neural outputs are temporally and cross-head correlated. The
  result is a reference-model score, not calibrated musical confidence.
- A single best path does not resolve competing meter or boundary modes. The
  component marginal and MAP probability are not per-boundary posteriors.
- A true erased-beat/half-speed collision in the same input representation is
  still not identifiable. This two-head audit supplies new bar evidence and
  must not be counted as an improvement on the old beat-only collision pair.

## Authored protocol

The seven timing controls reuse the original three 384-frame sections and
their unchanged beat-head bytes: constant, alternating weak constant, true
half speed, true double speed, alternating weak true double speed, non-octave
change and all-weak constant. They now add authored four-beat bar-head pulses.
Those section boundaries and periods exist **only in the scorer**, never in
the decoder call. Exact frame matches and per-tick authored-period agreement
are reported with full authored beat counts; edge omissions are not removed
from the denominator.

Additional controls are flat heads, fixed-seed independent head noise, a
192-frame unavailable gap, a flat-but-available middle interval, and a weak
tempo change after beat 19 (inside a four-beat bar). The first/last incomplete
bars in every control also exercise unknown edge handling. Development checks
are not preregistered real-music evaluation or evidence for training.
Three more controls add irregular weak offbeat pulses, weak three-beat bar
evidence, and a weak clock whose first tick is exactly frame zero.

Run `cargo run --locked --profile evaluation -p rhythm-map-eval --example
joint_clock`. The example emits a deterministic report on stdout and timings
on stderr; no model, recording, GPU, Python inference or private dataset is
required. Its exact reference graph is a correctness baseline, not a measured
production-performance claim.

## Result and next gate

**Rejected before real-cohort replay.** The first seven timing controls produce
the following MAP diagnostics (all authored beats remain in the denominator):

| Control | Exact matches / authored beats | Correct period / inferred ticks |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Constant, intact | 47 / 48 | 47 / 47 |
| Constant, alternating weak | 47 / 48 | 47 / 47 |
| True half speed | 39 / 40 | 39 / 39 |
| True double speed | 63 / 64 | 63 / 63 |
| True double speed, alternating weak | 47 / 64 | 31 / 47 |
| Non-octave change | 43 / 44 | 43 / 43 |
| Constant, all weak | 47 / 48 | 47 / 47 |

The weak true doubling again becomes constant 125 BPM. The MAP changes meter
from four to two in the middle, preserving the same physical bar boundaries
while omitting alternate beats. Thus correct absolute bar locations do not
resolve the tempo/meter ambiguity by themselves.

The independent, truth-assisted `joint_clock_diagnosis.py` reconstructs all
input hashes and the selected MAP score. It then compares a graph-legal authored
timing path on the **same** `[4,1132)` coverage, including the same truncated
final three-beat bar. For weak true doubling, authored minus selected:

| Score component | Difference |
| --- | ---: |
| Beat-head contextual evidence | +4.853289 |
| Whole-bar evidence | 0 |
| Duration transition prior | -17.491781 |
| Meter and edge priors | 0 |
| Total unnormalized path weight | -12.638492 |

The evidence still favors the denser correct timing; the normalized duration
prior overwhelms it in MAP path selection. This identifies a concrete objective
failure, not absent neural information. It does **not** prove that changing a
penalty or using marginals will solve it. The phase-zero cell geometry also
differs from the earlier given phase-four paths, so their score magnitudes are
not directly comparable.

Other failed or bounded controls:

- Flat heads give exactly zero evidence and no supported clock. Their diagnostic
  MAP still has arbitrary sparse ticks, all explicitly marked prior-only.
- Fixed-seed iid noise gives log ratio about +0.378: only about 1.46:1 reference
  odds, but the reference's zero-cutoff support flag is positive. Normalizing
  search is not a calibrated detection/abstention decision. A single noise draw
  does not estimate false-positive rate or justify a new threshold.
- The flat middle yields 72-frame periods (about 41.67 BPM) alongside 24-frame
  periods, despite authored constant tempo. It must not become a reported
  physical slowdown. Availability gaps instead split the graph cleanly.
- The weak intra-bar change is found with periods 24 and 32 and meter four.
- Every first-seven timing case misses the last beat and chooses a final
  three-beat bar, although the authored meter remains four. Reporting incomplete
  edge coverage is necessary but does not fix this edge/meter artifact.
- Irregular weak distractor pulses retain 24-frame timing (47/48 beats); weak
  three-beat evidence selects meter three in the body but truncates to two at
  the tail (47/48 beats). A frame-zero clock with complete edge bars recovers
  all 48 beats at period 24 and meter four with zero edge-reference frames.
  These successes do not offset the failures above.

Next test time-indexed state and decision semantics (including how probability
mass is assigned to paths with different beat counts), and partial-bar
marginalization with explicit unsupported states. Retain the successful
contextual evidence and exact enumeration checks; do not fix these failures
with a per-case threshold, a user strategy, meter restriction, or a sweep on
the retained music. No real-music comparison or training verdict is warranted
by this failed authored gate.
